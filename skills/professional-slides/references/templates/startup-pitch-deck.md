# Startup Pitch Deck

## Mandate

Use this template when founders must convince investors that a problem, product, market, team, and financing plan support the next stage of company building.

## Decision question

> Why should this company be funded now, and what milestones will the round buy?

## Delivery mode

Choose live pitch, investor pre-read, or short teaser. Live decks use larger type and fewer ideas per page. Pre-reads may carry more evidence and sources.

A live pitch normally omits a visible tracker and may omit a standalone executive summary when the opening sequence already makes the investment case clear.

## Thesis and scope

State the company stage, evidence cutoff, round context, and the decision the audience is being asked to make. Calibrate claims to the stage. Do not make early signals look like mature cohort proof.

Use [storylining](../storylining/index.md) and validate the [pre-authoring contract](../storylining/pre-authoring-contract.md) before production.

## Story structure

A full pitch usually covers:

1. problem and urgency;
2. product and user experience;
3. demand proof and customer outcome;
4. market and competitive position;
5. business model and go-to-market;
6. scale logic and key risks;
7. team;
8. round, milestones, and close.

Merge jobs when the story is short. Do not preserve template pages that add no proof.

## Navigation

Record `tracker.system: none` for a live pitch or short teaser. For an investor pre-read with three to six retained analytical chapters, use `sequential-circles` as the full state and `compact-number-strip` on governed slides. With seven or more retained chapters, use `split-contents` as the full state and `compact-label` on governed slides. Seed the approved tracker map with `Problem and product`, `Demand proof`, `Market and competition`, `Business model and go-to-market`, `Scale and risk`, `Team`, and `Round and milestones`; approved replacements become exact under the [template instantiation contract](index.md#instantiation-contract). Omit unused chapters from both the dot-dash and tracker map; do not add a tracker only to imitate a longer deck.

## Analytical jobs

### Problem and product

Show the user problem, why existing options fail, and how the product changes the experience. Use a simple journey, product flow, or demonstration.

### Demand proof

Use the strongest evidence available: usage, growth, retention, conversion, revenue, customer outcomes, or qualified pipeline. Label company-reported or unaudited measures.

Do not treat downloads, press, or a few logos as retention.

### Market and competition

Define the reachable market and why now. Compare against real alternatives on the criteria buyers care about. Avoid generic TAM circles and unsupported claims that there are no competitors.

### Business model and go-to-market

Show how use becomes revenue and contribution. Explain pricing, acquisition, sales motion, supply, channels, or network dynamics as relevant.

### Scale and risk

Show what improves with scale and what may break first. Tie risks to milestones rather than adding a generic risk matrix.

### Team

Name the people, relevant evidence of ability, and the role each person plays in the next stage. Logos and biographies alone are not team proof.

### Round and milestones

State the raise when known and show what it buys. Link capital to measurable product, growth, operating, or market milestones. Do not present an unsupported budget as a financing plan.

## Evidence

Separate fact, company claim, estimate, and inference. Cite public or supplied evidence. Do not invent traction, economics, market share, customers, team history, or funding details.

## Page composition

Use the [open composition model](../composition/index.md). Prefer one large proof per page. Use a chart when the evidence is quantitative, a product flow when the experience matters, and a simple causal diagram when the mechanism matters.

Avoid repeated cards, tiny text, decorative screenshots, and unnecessary section dividers.

## Failure checks

Reject the deck when:

- the problem or product is vague;
- traction is not dated or sourced;
- the market is only a headline TAM;
- the business model is missing;
- risks are generic;
- team credibility is not shown;
- the round lacks milestones;
- the deck uses dense pre-read layouts for a live pitch;
- visual repetition replaces evidence.

## Acceptance check

The deck makes the problem, product, demand proof, market, business model, scale logic, risks, team, round, and milestones clear. The exact final artifact passes rendered QA.
