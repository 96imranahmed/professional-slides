# Category Overview

This is a specialized composition profile. Select [`decomposition`](decomposition.md) when the categories are parts of one system or [`comparison and options`](comparison-options.md) when the audience evaluates peer categories, then use this profile for three to five concise icon-led fields.

## Use when

The audience must understand a small MECE taxonomy before later pages examine its parts. Suitable examples include diligence branches, customer needs, value levers, operating capabilities, risk families, or programme workstreams.

## Content contract

- three to five mutually exclusive categories that collectively answer the parent question;
- one concise label, one sentence of scope, and optionally one proof point per category;
- one consistent icon or logo treatment from [`icons-and-logos`](../components/icons-and-logos.md);
- one parent statement explaining why the taxonomy matters.

## Layout

Use one row for three or four compact categories, two rows for four or five categories with more evidence, or one left-hand parent field plus aligned child rows when hierarchy matters. Use open fields with top rules or whitespace before boxed cards. Keep peers equal unless the narrative explicitly names one as dominant.

## Structural HTML reference

Use the inline-SVG category-row specimen in [`icons-and-logos.md`](../components/icons-and-logos.md#structural-html-reference). For the hierarchical variant, place the parent statement in the first three grid columns and the category row in the remaining nine columns.

## Failure modes

Overlapping categories, generic icons, different accent colours for peers, slogans without scope, five categories that do not cover the parent question, and decorative category cards that consume more space than their evidence.

## Acceptance test

Removing any category creates a real gap, merging any two loses a decision-relevant distinction, the icons accelerate scanning without carrying unsupported meaning, and the labels recur consistently in later pages when the taxonomy becomes navigation.
