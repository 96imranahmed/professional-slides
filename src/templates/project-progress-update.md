# Project Progress Update

## Mandate

Use this template for a sponsor, steering committee, board, regulator, public authority, or programme leadership team deciding whether a major programme remains on track, whether forecast outcomes remain credible, and what intervention or decision is required now. It is designed for programmes whose outcomes depend on linked schedule, cost, benefits, readiness, assurance, stakeholder, and delivery evidence rather than a list of independent workstream updates.

The root question is: **What has changed since the last approved review, does the programme still achieve its committed outcomes, what now controls the forecast, and which decisions protect or recover the case?**

The governing thought must connect delivered and accepted progress, forecast outcome, critical-path evidence, residual risk or readiness, and the sponsor action required. A status deck is not coherent when it reports activity without explaining how the current state changes the programme outcome or decision.

## Delivery mode and completeness

Classify the delivery mode before building the hypothesis tree and record it in the brief, pre-authoring contract, template-coverage ledger, and document identification:

- **Executive programme pre-read:** the default for a substantive board, sponsor, regulator, or steering decision. It must reconcile the approved baseline to current actuals, expose the first binding critical-path constraint, show residual risk and readiness, produce an independent forecast or evidenced forecast assessment, and state the decisions or interventions required.
- **Live steering update:** a shorter presentation used in a recurring governance meeting. It may compress stable baseline and method material, but it must still state what changed, what controls the outcome, which thresholds were crossed, and what decision is required. Meeting familiarity does not justify omitting the forecast consequence or decision evidence.
- **Recovery or intervention review:** a decision pack for a programme outside tolerance or approaching a material gate. It must compare the current forecast with the approved baseline and viable recovery cases, identify the causal constraints, quantify contingency and residual exposure, and specify the sponsor choices, owners, timing, and stop or escalation triggers.
- **Retrospective programme review:** a reconstruction of a completed or historic programme decision. Freeze each relevant reporting cutoff, distinguish contemporaneous evidence from hindsight, and evaluate whether the forecast, risks, and governance decisions were supportable at the time rather than rewriting the record from final outcomes.

When the evidence cannot support the requested mode, stop before finished-slide authoring. Obtain the missing baseline, schedule, cost, benefits, risk, readiness, or assurance evidence; explicitly narrow the mode; or record the unresolved decision limitation. Do not present a narrative status summary as a complete executive programme pre-read.

Before storylining, create a template-coverage ledger with one row for every retained or omitted slide job. Record the delivery mode, hypothesis branch, evidence package, planned page or explicit merge, and decision-relevant rationale for omission. A missing-data statement identifies a gap; it does not complete the analytical job.

## How to use this outline

Treat every row in the blueprint as a named communication job, not a mandatory page count. Retain the jobs needed to prove or disprove the approved programme thesis, merge adjacent jobs only when one evidence structure can answer both without weakening the action title, and move audit or lookup detail to the appendix.

- **Core:** normally required in an executive programme pre-read.
- **Conditional:** required when material to the governing thought, tolerance position, forecast, gate, or sponsor decision.
- **Transition:** uses the approved tracker state at a material change in the audience's governing question.
- **Appendix:** preserves reconciliation, method, and source detail without interrupting the decision story.

Every retained analytical slide must map to one approved hypothesis branch, one dot in the dot-dash, one action title, one evidence package, one canonical slide type selected through the [`slide-type router`](../slide-types/index.md), and one [`evidence composition`](../slide-types/evidence-compositions.md). Do not define programme-specific slide types or shorthand. The [`pre-authoring contract`](../storylining/pre-authoring-contract.md) owns the executable handoff and validation.

## Thesis and scope contract

Build the programme thesis across five linked analytical tests:

1. **Outcome and baseline integrity:** intended outcomes, scope, approved schedule, budget, benefits, quality or service thresholds, tolerance, and change control form one coherent reference case.
2. **Delivered progress:** accepted milestones, deliverables, workstream outputs, expenditure, benefits, and quality evidence reconcile to the baseline and reporting cutoff.
3. **Critical path and dependencies:** the few activities, interfaces, decisions, resources, or external conditions controlling forecast outcomes are identified through schedule and causal evidence.
4. **Residual risk and readiness:** mitigated exposure, operational readiness, assurance, stakeholder, commercial, technical, and transition evidence support the next gate and reveal what can still invalidate it.
5. **Forecast and intervention:** an evidence-backed current forecast and decision-relevant recovery or downside cases translate the first four tests into sponsor action, ownership, timing, and triggers.

The programme thesis must reconcile these tests into one answer. A strong outcome metric cannot compensate for an unproven critical path; activity completion cannot substitute for accepted deliverables; an unchanged headline forecast cannot hide depleted contingency or declining confidence; and a mitigation plan does not remove risk unless residual exposure is assessed.

Do not let the template replace project controls, cost assurance, safety, engineering, commercial, legal, operational-readiness, or regulatory work. Include those topics only through the evidence and consequences required for the programme decision, and preserve the owning workstream's approved definitions and judgments.

## Chapter and tracker contract

Derive chapter and tracker labels from the approved hypothesis tree, dot-dash, governance language, and decision sequence; this template does not prescribe label wording. Record each approved label and any compact form once in the tracker record and reuse it unchanged across contents, transitions, analytical headers, and cross-references.

For a new multi-chapter executive pre-read, place a standalone [`executive synthesis`](../slide-types/executive-synthesis.md) after the cover and establish the complete navigation map through the [`tracker system`](../components/trackers/index.md). Repeat the same full-state tracker only at material changes in the governing question. For an existing-deck revision, preserve the complete as-is dot-dash and do not add missing new-deck structure outside the authorized scope; record the recommendation instead.

A short live update may use a compact map or running tracker state when orientation would otherwise be lost. Tracker labels describe navigation, not status. Workstream health marks, milestones, gates, and stage labels remain evidence fields and must not become substitute chapter names.

The default narrative progression for a new executive pre-read is:

1. answer and decision;
2. approved baseline and change since the last review;
3. delivered and accepted progress;
4. critical path and binding dependencies;
5. residual risk, readiness, and assurance;
6. forecast, recovery or downside, and sponsor decisions; and
7. appendix evidence and reconciliations.

Chapter conclusions must state what the branch changes in the programme thesis and why the next branch is necessary. Do not end a chapter with a recap of activities performed.

## Evidence and measurement contract

Freeze the reporting cutoff, approved baseline version, current forecast version, change-control state, currency, price basis, schedule calendar, benefits basis, and confidence method before analysis. Preserve source-system identifiers and reconciliation logic in the evidence ledger.

For every reported measure, state the relevant unit, period, population or scope, actual-versus-forecast state, source, and material transformation. Use the following distinctions consistently:

- **Activity started or completed** is not an accepted deliverable unless the acceptance authority, criteria, date, and evidence are recorded.
- **Percentage complete** requires a defined numerator, denominator, weighting basis, evidence of completion, and relationship to schedule or value delivered.
- **Milestone achieved** requires the approved milestone definition, planned and actual date, acceptance state, and any remaining condition.
- **Cost incurred, committed, forecast, and authorized** are separate states; explain accrual, contingency, escalation, foreign exchange, risk allowance, and scope basis where material.
- **Benefit delivered, enabled, forecast, and at risk** are separate states; identify the beneficiary, measure, baseline, timing, dependency, and attribution boundary.
- **Risk exposure before mitigation** does not establish the current decision position; show residual exposure, trigger, mitigation status, owner, timing, and affected outcome.
- **Readiness** requires a named gate, criteria, evidence owner, assessed state, exceptions, waiver or acceptance authority, and consequence of failure.

Use progress circles only for real numeric completion and [`comparison indicators`](../components/comparison-indicators.md) only when their scale and semantics are reproducible. Use the [`project-status profile`](../slide-types/project-status.md) only after selecting its parent slide type through the router. Apply chart and table rules through the canonical [`chart router`](../charts/index.md), not through template-local chart instructions.

## Programme forecast and decision model

Maintain one integrated logic from the approved baseline to the current decision. The model may be held in the programme's native planning or cost system, but the deck must expose the decision-relevant bridge:

- approved scope and outcome commitments;
- accepted actual progress and remaining work;
- schedule logic, calendars, constraints, float, and interfaces;
- actual, committed, forecast, and contingent cost;
- benefits timing and dependency on delivery or adoption;
- risk and opportunity events with timing and affected drivers;
- readiness and assurance gates;
- resource, supplier, stakeholder, regulatory, and decision dependencies;
- base, recovery, and downside assumptions where they could change the sponsor action; and
- the decision threshold, trigger, owner, deadline, and consequence of delay.

Do not create arbitrary percentage downside cases. Derive scenarios from named events, constraints, productivity, sequence, approval, funding, procurement, readiness, or adoption assumptions. Keep common drivers and outputs aligned and reconcile each case to the same approved baseline.

## Variants

### Live recurring update

Compress stable baseline and method material, use the larger live-presentation type roles, and focus the core story on change since the last review, binding constraint, residual exposure, current forecast, and decision. Do not turn the deck into a repeated dashboard whose audience must infer why the status changed.

### Board or sponsor pre-read

Retain explicit baseline reconciliation, critical-path evidence, forecast bridge, residual risk, readiness, recovery or downside, and decision pages. Use denser evidence only within the executive pre-read mode and keep the action-title spine readable as a standalone memo.

### Regulator or public-authority review

Preserve statutory, assurance, public-outcome, safety, accessibility, consultation, and evidence boundaries where they affect the governing decision. Do not make legal or regulatory conclusions outside the owning authority; state the programme consequence and source the approved judgment.

### Recovery review

Lead with the current breach or threatened outcome, then show cause, viable cases, required intervention, contingency, decision rights, and triggers. Do not spend the opening pages restating a baseline the decision makers already accept unless the baseline itself is disputed.

### Existing-deck revision

Create the complete as-is dot-dash and validated pre-authoring contract before mutation. Preserve established tracker and header systems, recommend missing new-deck structure without forcing it, and update the target contract only for authorized structural changes.

## Adjacent workstreams and handoffs

- Route engineering sufficiency, technical acceptance, safety, legal enforceability, commercial claims, accounting treatment, procurement compliance, and regulatory judgment to their owning workstreams.
- Bring an adjacent finding into the programme story only through its evidenced effect on outcome, schedule, cost, benefits, readiness, residual risk, or decision.
- Preserve the owning workstream's exact status, confidence, caveat, authority, and date; do not translate a qualified judgment into a categorical programme claim.
- Identify the handoff owner, required evidence, timing, and decision consequence when another workstream must resolve an open condition.

## Failure checks

- the deck reports activity rather than accepted outcomes or decision-relevant change;
- current status cannot be reconciled to an approved baseline and reporting cutoff;
- every workstream appears equally important despite a binding critical path;
- schedule, cost, benefit, risk, or readiness states use inconsistent definitions or periods;
- percentage complete, confidence, health, or readiness scores have no reproducible basis;
- the forecast changes without a causal bridge, or remains unchanged despite depleted contingency and worsening evidence;
- scenarios are arbitrary percentage adjustments rather than named events, constraints, choices, or mitigations;
- risks are listed before mitigation or without residual exposure, trigger, owner, affected driver, and decision consequence;
- readiness is reduced to colored status without criteria, evidence, authority, and exceptions;
- recovery actions lack a quantified mechanism, owner, timing, dependency, or stop trigger;
- the sponsor decision appears only in the close and is not established by the preceding analysis;
- adjacent workstream opinions are presented as programme facts without source authority or qualification;
- lookup schedules crowd the core story while a binding constraint or decision remains implicit; or
- a page uses a template-local slide-type alias instead of a direct reference to the canonical slide-type owner.

## Acceptance check

The deck passes only when the intended audience can identify, from the final artifact and without narration:

- the approved baseline, reporting cutoff, and material changes since the prior review;
- what has actually been delivered and accepted;
- the first binding critical-path constraint and its quantified consequence;
- current cost, schedule, benefit, risk, and readiness posture on reconciled definitions;
- the evidence-backed forecast, confidence, and viable recovery or downside cases;
- residual exposure after mitigation and the next gate or trigger;
- the exact decision, owner, deadline, and consequence of delay; and
- one governing programme thesis preserved across the opening answer, branch conclusions, action plan, and close.

The template-coverage ledger, validated pre-authoring contract, source ledger, final editable artifact, cross-slide consistency pass, and rendered QA must agree. Missing-data statements, attractive status visuals, or a complete activity list do not compensate for an unresolved programme decision.
