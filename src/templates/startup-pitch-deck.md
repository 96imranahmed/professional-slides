# Startup Pitch Deck

## Mandate

Use this template for investors deciding whether a startup warrants a meeting, further diligence, or funding and whether the proposed round can support the next value-creation milestone. It is designed for companies whose investment case depends on a causal link among customer pain, product advantage, demand evidence, market reach, scalable economics, team capability, and the use of capital rather than a conventional sequence of company facts.

The root question is: **Why should this company win now, what evidence shows it can become a valuable business, and what will the requested capital prove or unlock?**

The governing thought must connect an urgent customer problem, a differentiated solution, evidence of demand, a credible scaling mechanism, and the purpose of the raise. A pitch is not coherent when these appear as separate company facts without one causal investment thesis.

## Delivery mode and completeness

Classify the delivery mode before building the hypothesis tree and record it in the brief, pre-authoring contract, template-coverage ledger, and document identification:

- **Live first-meeting pitch:** the default for a substantive investor presentation. It must establish the investment thesis, problem and timing, product mechanism, stage-appropriate demand proof, market and competitive position, scaling model, team fit, round, milestone use of funds, and next investor action. It should normally work in roughly ten to fifteen main-story slides without depending on speaker notes for the core proof.
- **Investor pre-read:** a self-contained document for review without narration. It retains the complete investment logic, adds the evidence, definitions, and caveats required to interpret each claim, and routes lookup detail to a governed appendix. It must remain a pitch rather than expanding into a commercial due-diligence report.
- **Short teaser or demo-day pitch:** a deliberately incomplete acquisition document intended to secure a conversation. It may compress market, model, team, and financing evidence, but it must still state the customer problem, differentiated mechanism, strongest stage-appropriate proof, reason to believe, and explicit next action. Record the omitted full-pitch jobs in the coverage ledger rather than treating them as completed.
- **Retrospective fundraising reconstruction:** a review of a historic raise or investment case. Freeze the information date, distinguish evidence available at the time from hindsight, and evaluate whether the claims and milestones were supportable then.

When the evidence cannot support the requested mode, stop before finished-slide authoring. Obtain the missing customer, product, traction, market, model, team, financing, or milestone evidence; explicitly narrow the delivery mode; or record the unresolved investment limitation. Do not present a polished narrative as a complete live pitch or investor pre-read when its decisive proof is absent.

Before storylining, create a template-coverage ledger with one row for every retained or omitted slide job. Record the delivery mode, hypothesis branch, evidence package, planned page or explicit merge, and investment-relevant rationale for omission. A missing-data statement identifies a gap; it does not complete the analytical job.

## How to use this outline

Treat every row in the blueprint as a named communication job, not a mandatory page count. Retain the jobs needed to prove or disprove the investment thesis, merge adjacent jobs only when one evidence structure can answer both without weakening the action title, and move diligence or lookup detail to the appendix.

- **Core:** normally required in a live first-meeting pitch or investor pre-read.
- **Conditional:** required when material to the investment thesis, stage, business model, round, or next financing gate.
- **Transition:** uses the approved tracker state only when the pitch is long enough to require navigation.
- **Appendix:** preserves definitions, reconciliations, source detail, and diligence support without interrupting the investment story.

Every retained analytical slide must map to one approved hypothesis branch, one dot in the dot-dash, one action title, one evidence package, one canonical slide type selected through the [`slide-type router`](../slide-types/index.md), and one [`evidence composition`](../slide-types/evidence-compositions.md). Do not define pitch-specific slide types or shorthand. The [`pre-authoring contract`](../storylining/pre-authoring-contract.md) owns the executable handoff and validation.

## Thesis and scope contract

Build the investment thesis across five linked tests:

1. **Problem and timing:** a valuable customer problem exists, current alternatives fail in a specific way, and market, behavioral, regulatory, or technology conditions make the opportunity urgent now.
2. **Solution and advantage:** the product solves the problem materially better and has a credible mechanism for sustained differentiation, learning, distribution, cost, data, workflow, brand, network, or regulatory advantage.
3. **Demand proof:** adoption, usage, retention, revenue, pipeline, customer evidence, or validated technical and commercial milestones demonstrate real pull at the company's stage.
4. **Scale model:** reachable market, business model, go-to-market motion, economics, operating requirements, and roadmap can compound into a large and defensible outcome.
5. **Team and raise:** the team is unusually suited to resolve the critical execution risks and the requested capital funds explicit milestones that create value and support the next financing or self-sustaining state.

The investment thesis must reconcile these tests into one answer. A large market cannot compensate for weak customer proof; growth without retention or economic context does not establish durable demand; product novelty does not prove an advantage; and a strong team does not make an undefined use of capital investable.

Do not let the template replace legal, technical, security, regulatory, accounting, tax, commercial due diligence, market research, or financial-model assurance. Include those topics only through evidence and consequences material to the investment decision, and preserve the owning workstream's approved definitions and judgments.

## Chapter and tracker contract

Derive chapter and tracker labels from the approved investment hypothesis tree, dot-dash, audience language, and decision sequence; this template does not prescribe label wording. Record each approved label and any compact form once in the tracker record and reuse it unchanged across contents, transitions, analytical headers, and cross-references.

A new startup pitch does not automatically require a standalone executive synthesis or contents page. The pre-authoring contract may mark them `not_required` when the opening investment-thesis page and short live sequence provide enough orientation. A longer investor pre-read may use a compact decision map and the canonical [`tracker system`](../components/trackers/index.md). Repeat a full-state tracker only at material changes in the governing question; do not decorate every pitch slide with section labels.

The default narrative progression is:

1. investment thesis and reason to engage now;
2. customer problem, timing, and product mechanism;
3. demand proof and customer outcomes;
4. market reach and competitive position;
5. business model, go-to-market, economics, and scale path;
6. team fit, round, milestone use of funds, and next investor action; and
7. appendix evidence for diligence.

Chapter conclusions must state what the evidence changes in the investment thesis and why the next branch is necessary. Do not end a chapter with a recap of features, activities, or company history.

## Delivery and density contract

Default to the live-pitch density mode: larger type, one claim, one proof, and minimal lookup detail per page. An investor pre-read may use the executive-presentation mode, but it must not inherit commercial-due-diligence density merely because both decks address investors.

Use the [`metric-page profile`](../slide-types/metric-page.md) only for one to three stage-defining values whose definitions, periods, and comparisons are explicit. Use the [`market-landscape profile`](../slide-types/market-landscape.md) only when it establishes a real category, competitor, customer, partner, or channel pattern. Use icon-led categories only when they form a MECE product, customer, or use-of-funds taxonomy.

The deck should vary among product or mechanism evidence, decisive metric or chart pages, customer proof, a market or competitive landscape, a scale model, a team page, and a milestone-based raise page. Variation must follow the investment argument; do not manufacture it with cards, decorative photos, unsupported logos, or repeated metric blocks.

## Stage and evidence calibration

Freeze the information date, company stage, product state, geography, customer definition, metric dictionary, revenue-recognition basis, pipeline stages, currency, forecast version, round target, and financing assumptions before analysis. Preserve source identifiers and transformations in the evidence ledger.

For every metric, state the relevant definition, unit, period, population or cohort, actual-versus-target state, source, and material transformation. Apply these distinctions consistently:

- **Signed, contracted, invoiced, recognized, collected, recurring, and annualized revenue** are separate states.
- **User, account, seat, customer, location, and transaction** denominators are not interchangeable.
- **Booking, waitlist, letter of intent, pilot, proof of concept, qualified pipeline, and contracted customer** represent different strengths of demand evidence.
- **Gross, logo, revenue, and cohort retention** require explicit definitions, dates, and populations; cumulative customer counts do not establish retention.
- **Target, plan, forecast, scenario, and aspiration** must never be styled or narrated as actual performance.
- **Market size, reachable segment, and near-term commercial pipeline** answer different questions and must not be added together.
- **Product demonstration, technical benchmark, customer outcome, and commercial adoption** are different evidence classes; explain which claim each supports.

Calibrate proof to stage without lowering the standard of honesty:

- **Pre-product or pre-revenue:** emphasize observed customer pain, validated willingness to change or pay, product or technical proof, founder-market fit, credible build and distribution milestones, and falsifiable next tests. Do not substitute waitlist volume or generic market growth for demand proof.
- **Early revenue:** emphasize activation, repeated usage, retention, conversion, customer outcomes, sales-cycle evidence, pricing, gross margin logic, and repeatability signals. Separate founder-led wins from a scalable motion.
- **Scaling:** emphasize cohort durability, net revenue retention where relevant, efficient acquisition, payback, gross margin, pipeline conversion, expansion, market reach, operating leverage, and the constraints that capital will remove.

## Market, traction, economics, and milestone model

Maintain one integrated logic from the initial customer problem to the financing decision. The model may live in the company's native systems, but the deck must expose the decision-relevant bridge:

- target customer, use case, buying trigger, current alternative, and economic or experiential pain;
- product mechanism, outcome, adoption requirement, and source of advantage;
- acquisition channels, funnel stages, conversion, sales cycle, implementation, activation, retention, expansion, and churn;
- price, revenue model, frequency, gross margin, variable delivery cost, acquisition cost, payback, and working-capital needs where material;
- reachable segment, account or user counts, penetration logic, and expansion path;
- hiring, product, regulatory, technical, data, supply, geographic, and operating dependencies;
- base, upside, and downside assumptions only when they alter the funding decision or milestone plan; and
- round size, runway, milestone costs, evidence gates, next financing trigger, and contingency.

Do not create arbitrary percentage scenarios or top-down market shares. Derive cases from named customer, conversion, pricing, retention, capacity, hiring, product, regulatory, or financing assumptions. Keep the same definitions and driver logic across the traction story, market argument, financial forecast, and use-of-funds plan.

## Variants

### Short teaser or demo-day pitch

Lead with the investment thesis, customer problem, product mechanism, strongest proof, scale potential, team reason to believe, and next action. Explicitly record omitted full-pitch jobs in the coverage ledger. Do not imply that a short acquisition document satisfies a full investor review.

### Investor pre-read

Retain the full causal investment logic, metric definitions, market reconciliation, competitive evidence, go-to-market and economic model, risks, round, milestone use of funds, and diligence appendix. Use denser evidence only within the executive-presentation mode and keep the action-title spine readable as a standalone memo.

### Pre-product or pre-revenue pitch

Replace unavailable traction jobs with the strongest observed customer, technical, product, distribution, or willingness-to-pay evidence and explicit falsifiable milestones. Do not invent pseudo-metrics or describe targets as validation.

### Scaling-stage pitch

Prioritize cohort durability, revenue quality, acquisition efficiency, expansion, gross margin, operating leverage, market reach, and the constraints the round removes. Compress product explanation only when the mechanism and advantage remain clear to a new investor.

### Existing-deck revision

Create the complete as-is dot-dash and validated pre-authoring contract before mutation. Preserve the established tracker and header systems, recommend missing new-deck structure without forcing it, and update the target contract only for authorized structural changes.

## Adjacent workstreams and handoffs

- Route legal enforceability, intellectual property, technical architecture, security, privacy, regulatory status, accounting treatment, taxation, capitalization, and formal market assurance to their owning workstreams.
- Bring an adjacent finding into the pitch only through its evidenced effect on the investment thesis, milestone plan, economics, risk, financing need, or investor decision.
- Preserve the owning workstream's exact status, confidence, caveat, authority, and date; do not translate a qualified judgment into a categorical company claim.
- Identify the handoff owner, required evidence, timing, and investment consequence when another workstream must resolve an open condition.

## Failure checks

- the deck follows a conventional pitch order but does not express one causal investment thesis;
- the opening is a slogan or mission statement that does not identify the customer, reason to believe, scale mechanism, or financing purpose;
- the product section is a feature tour without a customer outcome or proof of advantage;
- actuals, targets, forecasts, pilots, pipeline, waitlists, and anecdotes are visually or verbally conflated;
- traction uses cumulative or vanity figures without cohorts, conversion, retention, scope, or definitions;
- customer logos or quotes lack governed provenance or do not prove the claim attached to them;
- market size is disconnected from the initial segment, price, reachability, go-to-market motion, or operating plan;
- the competitive page omits current alternatives or uses arbitrary axes designed to make the company appear uniquely favorable;
- the growth model cannot be reconciled to acquisition, retention, price, margin, capacity, hiring, and cash assumptions;
- unit economics mix incompatible cohorts, periods, attribution boundaries, or mature-state assumptions;
- the team is missing, reduced to biographies, or disconnected from the specific execution risks in the thesis;
- the raise is a departmental budget list rather than a set of evidence-producing milestones;
- the deck hides decisive technical, regulatory, customer, financing, or execution risk;
- the close repeats the mission but does not state the round purpose and specific next investor action;
- appendix detail introduces definitions or claims that conflict with the core story; or
- a page uses a template-local slide-type alias instead of a direct reference to the canonical slide-type owner.

## Acceptance check

The deck passes only when the intended investor can identify, from the final artifact and without private narration:

- the defined customer, valuable problem, failed current alternative, and reason the opportunity matters now;
- the product mechanism, demonstrated customer outcome, and credible source of advantage;
- the strongest stage-appropriate demand proof and the exact status, definition, period, and limitation of each decisive metric;
- the reachable initial segment, expansion logic, and evidence-based competitive position;
- how acquisition, retention, price, margin, capacity, and operating milestones create a scalable model;
- why this team is suited to resolve the remaining execution risks;
- the round, runway basis, milestone use of funds, next financing or sustainable-state gate, and material contingency;
- the major unresolved risks and next falsifiable tests; and
- one governing investment thesis preserved across the opening, branch conclusions, milestone plan, and close.

The template-coverage ledger, validated pre-authoring contract, source ledger, metric dictionary, final editable artifact, cross-slide consistency pass, and rendered QA must agree. Missing-data statements, attractive product imagery, market-size headlines, or a complete feature list do not compensate for an unresolved investment decision.
