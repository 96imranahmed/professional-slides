# Copy

Write like a clear executive memo. Every sentence must add a claim, evidence, interpretation, decision, action, source, or necessary navigation.

## Action titles

State the answer, not the topic. Use the most decision-relevant supported comparison, magnitude, period, segment, or threshold when it sharpens the claim. Narrow the title when the evidence is weaker than the wording.

## Body copy

Keep only the detail needed to understand the exhibit. Use plain words, short sentences, and explicit uncertainty. Prefer direct statements such as `Public evidence does not show retention` over vague language such as `Further work may be required`.

Do not expose planning language such as `Slide job`, `Exhibit logic`, `So what`, `Hypothesis`, or `Decision relevance` unless the deck is teaching that method.

## Labels

Keep labels only when they help the audience decode data, navigation, timing, ownership, units, or scenario state.

A role label on a callout or terminal action surface is presumptively slop. Delete labels such as `Recommendation`, `Key takeaway`, `Implication`, or `Decision gate` when the sentence and placement already communicate the role. Never render the recommendation and its call to action in separate text-box surfaces.

## Punctuation

Audience-facing copy must contain zero em dashes. There are no quotation, official-name, source-wording, or parenthetical exceptions. Use commas, full stops, parentheses, or a clear connector instead.

Do not use repeated colons, semicolons, ellipses, or parenthetical asides to create a house style.

## Avoid generic AI copy

Delete inflated or portable phrasing that could fit any deck, including repeated `not just X, but Y` constructions, false contrasts, rhetorical questions, and empty claims such as `transformative`, `best in class`, or `significant opportunity` without proof.

## Copy QA

1. Delete any sentence that does not change the audience's understanding or decision.
2. Check that titles are supported by the exhibit.
3. Remove redundant labels and repeated framing.
4. Read the slide aloud and simplify awkward phrasing.
5. As the final copy QA check, scan the exact final artifact for the Unicode em dash character and repeat the scan until it returns zero matches.
