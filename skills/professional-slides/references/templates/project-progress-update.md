# Project progress update

## Mandate

Use when a sponsor, board or steering group must decide whether a programme remains on track and what action is needed. Choose the delivery mode first: live update (shorter, lower density), executive pre-read (more evidence and sources), public-authority review, or recovery review.

## Decision question

> Is the approved outcome still achievable, what now controls it, and what must the sponsor decide?

## Page table

| Page | Job | Exhibit | Evidence it owes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cover | Name the programme, reporting body and cutoff | Cover | Reporting cutoff, perimeter, forecast basis |
| Executive summary | State the overall answer and the sponsor's decision | Executive synthesis | Answer, proof, decision consequences, sponsor action |
| Baseline and forecast movement | Show the approved baseline against the current position | Timeline, milestone view or variance chart | Baseline, current position, material variance, defined denominators |
| Delivered progress | Show what is now complete | Milestone or deliverable exhibit | Completion evidence rather than percentages without a denominator |
| Critical path | Name the first binding constraint | Dependency diagram or dated lanes | The constraint, its dependencies, and the date or outcome it controls |
| Residual risk and readiness | Show what remains after mitigation | Status table with written triggers | Evidence thresholds for readiness, safety, operations, people, systems, assurance |
| Forecast and downside | State the latest forecast and its basis | Scenario or range exhibit | Forecast window against fixed date, consequence of slippage, failed tests, dependency delay |
| Actions and decisions | Separate management actions from sponsor decisions | Action table | Owner, timing, completion evidence, the decision each action enables |
| Appendix | Reference schedules and source record | Tables | Dated, attributable programme evidence reconciled across views |

A list of workstreams is not a critical-path analysis, and the executive summary is a synthesis rather than a status dashboard.

## Evidence

Use dated, attributable programme evidence and cite every page. Reconcile numbers across milestones, workstreams, risks and forecast views. Distinguish actual, forecast, target and retrospective context. Where accountability has not been supplied, record the gap and require a governance-confirmed assignment before execution rather than naming a presumed owner. Traffic-light labels carry their measure and threshold, or give way to the evidence, trigger, consequence and owner.

## Failure checks

Reject when: the baseline or cutoff is unclear; progress appears without the first binding constraint; risk is listed without residual consequence; forecast and target are mixed; colours substitute for evidence; execution-ready actions lack owners or completion evidence; the executive summary is a tracker; one generic layout carries most pages; sources are missing.

## Acceptance check

Baseline, progress, critical path, residual risk, forecast consequence, triggers, owners and sponsor decisions are all clear, and the exported deck passes the page gates and the review.
