# Commercial Due Diligence

## Mandate

Use this template for a buyer, investor, lender, board, or management team evaluating the durability and forward performance of a target's commercial model. It provides a complete default structure for a full commercial due-diligence deck; it is not a requirement to manufacture every page when the evidence or decision does not warrant it.

The root question is: **Can the target sustain and grow attractive revenue in the realistically reachable market, why will customers continue to choose it, and what evidence, downside, or condition should change the transaction decision?**

The governing thought must combine four answers: whether the market is attractive, whether customer demand and competitive advantage are durable, whether the commercial engine and management plan are credible, and what those conclusions mean for the decision.

## Engagement mode and completeness

Classify the engagement before building the hypothesis tree. Use exactly one mode and state it in the brief, template-coverage ledger, evidence ledger, and document identification:

- **Full commercial due diligence:** the default whenever the user asks for a standard, complete, or unqualified commercial DD. It must reach evidence-backed conclusions across all five analytical tests, build an independent commercial base case and decision-relevant downside, and reconcile those findings into the transaction recommendation.
- **Red-flag commercial due diligence:** a deliberately compressed stop, reprice, or condition screen. Retain only the hypotheses that can change the decision, but still trace each retained risk to evidence, an affected commercial driver, a test, a threshold, and a transaction consequence. Label the work as red-flag CDD; do not present it as a complete commercial DD.
- **Preliminary public-source commercial screen:** a bounded pre-diligence assessment using secondary evidence before management data or primary research is available. Separate demonstrated facts from hypotheses and missing evidence, but do not substitute missing-data lists for market, customer, competitive, commercial-engine, or plan analysis. Label the work as a preliminary public-source screen; do not call it full commercial DD and do not issue an unconditional underwrite or bid recommendation.

When the requested mode is full commercial due diligence but the evidence base cannot support it, stop before finished-slide authoring and surface the gap. Either obtain the required management data and primary research, or explicitly agree a red-flag or preliminary-screen scope. Never silently downgrade a full CDD into a public-evidence risk screen while retaining the full-CDD title.

Before storylining, create a template-coverage ledger with one row for every core slide job. Record the mode, retained page or merged page, hypothesis branch, evidence package, and reason for any omission. In a full CDD, every core job must be retained or deliberately merged into a page that still preserves its distinct analytical test, evidence, and conclusion. “Data unavailable,” “public evidence is limited,” and “requires diligence” do not complete the job; they identify an evidence gap that must be resolved, scoped out, or reflected in the engagement mode.

## How to use this outline

Treat every row below as a named slide job. Retain the jobs required to prove or disprove the approved thesis, combine adjacent jobs only when one exhibit can answer both without weakening the action title, and move audit detail to the appendix. Do not retain a page because it is conventional or because data happens to be available.

- **Core:** normally required in a full commercial due-diligence deck.
- **Conditional:** required when the topic is material to the thesis, plan, valuation, or downside.
- **Transition:** repeat the same approved full-state tracker at each material chapter opening; do not invent a new divider style by chapter.
- **Appendix:** detailed support that preserves traceability without interrupting the core argument.

Each retained analytical slide must map to one branch of the approved hypothesis tree, one dot in the dot-dash, one falsifiable action title, one evidence package, one narrative archetype, and one evidence composition. Storylining remains governed by [`../storylining/index.md`](../storylining/index.md), [`../storylining/hypothesis-tree.md`](../storylining/hypothesis-tree.md), and [`../storylining/dot-dash.md`](../storylining/dot-dash.md).

Chapter labels alone do not prove template compliance. A chapter is complete only when its required analytical jobs establish a branch conclusion. Repeating “public data does not disclose” across several pages is one evidence-limitation finding, not five completed diligence branches.

## Thesis and scope contract

Build the thesis across five linked analytical tests:

1. **Market attractiveness:** category size, growth, structure, drivers, constraints, and realistically reachable segments support the plan.
2. **Customer quality:** needs, willingness to pay, purchase criteria, retention, expansion, concentration, and segment behavior support durable demand.
3. **Competitive position:** differentiation, share, win and loss behavior, substitutes, channel power, and defensibility support sustained performance.
4. **Commercial engine:** proposition, pricing, marketing, sales, channels, onboarding, service, acquisition, retention, expansion, and cost to serve convert demand into attractive economics.
5. **Plan and downside:** historical performance, forecast drivers, sensitivities, execution dependencies, and evidence gaps support or invalidate the case.

Treat valuation, synergies, transaction terms, and value creation as decision synthesis after the standalone case has been tested. Buyer-specific synergies must not rescue an unsupported standalone commercial thesis.

Commercial due diligence does not replace financial, legal, tax, technical, cyber, operational, regulatory, or environmental due diligence. Include an adjacent topic only through its material commercial consequence: capacity through lost sales or service levels, technology through adoption or switching, regulation through addressable demand or cost to serve, and operations through delivery performance or customer economics. Route accounting, legal enforceability, security controls, and integration design to their owning workstreams.

## Chapter and tracker contract

Use these chapter labels by default: **Market attractiveness**, **Customer quality**, **Competitive position**, **Commercial engine**, **Plan and downside**, and **Decision and value creation**.

Place a standalone executive synthesis immediately after the cover and a full contents tracker immediately after the synthesis. Repeat the same full-state tracker at material chapter transitions with the current chapter emphasized. Analytical pages omit tracker furniture by default; use a compact chapter label only when orientation genuinely requires it. Follow [`../components/trackers/index.md`](../components/trackers/index.md) for tracker state and geometry.

The default sequence is:

1. cover, executive synthesis, and contents;
2. optional mandate, method, evidence base, and target baseline;
3. market attractiveness;
4. customer quality;
5. competitive position;
6. commercial engine;
7. plan and downside;
8. decision and value creation; and
9. appendix.

Chapter-ending pages must state what the branch means for the commercial thesis and what the next chapter must resolve. They must not merely recap analyses completed.

## Required design vocabulary

Use the existing narrative archetypes; no CDD-specific narrative type is required. A CDD page may use a specialized exhibit, but its communication job still routes to one of these archetypes:

| Code | Narrative archetype | Use in commercial due diligence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `SYN` | [`Executive synthesis`](../slide-types/executive-synthesis.md) | Decision summaries, chapter conclusions, thesis reconciliation, and final recommendations. |
| `DEC` | [`Decomposition`](../slide-types/decomposition.md) | Market definitions, driver trees, customer segmentation, funnels, capability models, and causal breakdowns. |
| `CLI` | [`Chart-led insight`](../slide-types/chart-led-insight.md) | One dominant quantitative exhibit that proves the title. |
| `TLI` | [`Text-led implication`](../slide-types/text-led-implication.md) | Qualitative findings, evidence limitations, proposition logic, barriers, and implications when prose is the evidence. |
| `AWC` | [`Argument with chart`](../slide-types/argument-with-chart.md) | A quantitative exhibit plus an explanatory mechanism, qualification, or decision consequence. |
| `CMP` | [`Comparison and options`](../slide-types/comparison-options.md) | Segment comparisons, competitor matrices, scenario comparisons, scorecards, risks, and choices. |
| `PRO` | [`Process and roadmap`](../slide-types/process-roadmap.md) | Customer journeys, commercial processes, implementation sequences, and the 100-day plan. |
| `ACT` | [`Recommendation and action plan`](../slide-types/recommendation-action-plan.md) | Owner-led mitigations, transaction conditions, value-creation actions, and next steps. |

Append the evidence-composition code defined in [`../slide-types/evidence-compositions.md`](../slide-types/evidence-compositions.md): `S` for one dominant evidence field, `P` for parallel evidence fields, `PS` for parallel evidence with a distinct synthesis region, and `G` for a progressive evidence path. For example, `CLI-S` means one dominant chart-led insight and `CMP-PS` means parallel comparisons that resolve into one synthesis.

Required CDD exhibit patterns route as follows:

| Exhibit pattern | Default route | Required design behavior |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Geographic opportunity map | `CLI-S` when location is the proof; `AWC-G` when location feeds a causal commercial argument | Use a restrained base map, direct labels, one meaningful color scale, and a clearly defined metric, period, and geographic unit. Never use a decorative map. |
| Market-sizing triangulation | `CMP-PS` or `AWC-G` | Show top-down and bottom-up logic in comparable structures, reconcile them in one explicit range, and separate TAM, serviceable demand, and realistically reachable demand. |
| Customer or segment scorecard | `CMP-S` | Use consistent columns, common scales, explicit criteria, direct labels, and a visible implication; avoid ornamental badges and arbitrary traffic-light colors. |
| Commercial funnel | `DEC-S` for stage economics; `PRO-G` for handoffs and process | Label stage definitions, cohort or period, conversion, volume, velocity, leakage, and ownership; do not show a generic funnel without economics. |
| Retention or cohort exhibit | `CLI-S` or `AWC-S` | Define logo versus revenue retention, cohort start, observation window, censoring, and expansion treatment; use the chart as proof and reserve prose for mechanism or limitation. |
| Competitive landscape | `CMP-S` or `CMP-PS` | Define the comparison universe and selection criteria, distinguish direct competitors, substitutes, and in-house alternatives, and limit dimensions to those customers actually use. |
| Driver tree | `DEC-G` | Show the complete mathematical or causal bridge from market and customer drivers to revenue and margin, with management assumptions visibly distinguished from independent conclusions. |
| Historical bridge or waterfall | `CLI-S` | Reconcile beginning to ending value with mutually exclusive drivers and stable signs, units, periods, and totals; use [`../charts/waterfall.md`](../charts/waterfall.md). |
| Scenario comparison | `CMP-PS` | Keep common drivers and outputs aligned across cases, name each trigger, and identify the decision consequence rather than presenting arbitrary percentage changes. |
| Financial schedule | `CLI-S` when one trend is the message; `CMP-S` when cases or segments are compared | Use a readable table with explicit units, periods, subtotals, and sources; highlight only cells that prove the title. It is an exhibit choice, not a separate narrative archetype. |
| Risk register | `CMP-PS` for risk prioritization; `ACT-PS` for owner-led mitigation | Separate fact, likelihood, impact, trigger, mitigation, owner, timing, and transaction consequence. A list of generic risks is not a decision page. |
| Multi-chart evidence page | `CLI-P` or `CLI-PS` | Give each peer chart the same encoding grammar and make each panel answer one subordinate question. Do not create a dashboard archetype or fill space with metrics. |

## Evidence and research requirements

Start with the transaction or investment thesis and identify three to five assumptions whose failure would change the decision. For each hypothesis, define the management claim, confirming and disconfirming evidence, intended sources, confidence threshold, model consequence, and decision consequence before gathering evidence.

Combine secondary research, management data, expert evidence, and primary customer or channel research when material and available. Define the relevant population and segmentation before selecting participants. Disclose sample size, recruitment source, coverage, bias, and inference limits. Distinguish independently recruited participants from management-provided references, and never present a small qualitative sample as statistical prevalence.

Maintain traceability from hypothesis to question, evidence, finding, confidence, model assumption, recommendation, and action. Separate verified facts, management claims, estimates, extrapolations, scenarios, contradictions, and unresolved tests. Every chart and table must state its metric, unit, period, population, source, and important limitation.

## Commercial-plan requirements

Build an independent driver tree rather than reproducing management's forecast. Expose the relevant market, share, customer additions, retention, expansion, price, volume, mix, capacity, channel productivity, acquisition cost, margin, and cost-to-serve drivers. Reconcile historical actuals before forecasting and make management assumptions visibly distinct from independently supported assumptions.

Use one supported base case and only the downside or upside cases that could change the decision. State the named trigger, affected drivers, timing, and decision consequence for every scenario. Do not create scenarios by applying uniform percentage changes to revenue or EBITDA without a causal bridge.

## Context variants

- **Buy-side commercial due diligence:** emphasize the standalone thesis, valuation-critical assumptions, independent base case, downside protection, and conditions for proceeding.
- **Vendor commercial due diligence:** present a defensible market and commercial fact base, disclose limitations, anticipate buyer challenges, and avoid advocacy that outruns evidence.
- **Red-flag commercial due diligence:** retain only the hypotheses and pages that can stop, reprice, or condition the decision; show unresolved tests and confidence prominently.
- **Lender commercial due diligence:** emphasize revenue resilience, concentration, churn, cyclicality, downside liquidity implications, covenant headroom, and financing sensitivity while keeping the analysis commercial rather than duplicating financial diligence.
- **Growth-equity commercial due diligence:** emphasize category expansion, product-market fit by segment, repeatability of acquisition, retention and expansion, capital efficiency, leadership capacity, and the evidence supporting the next stage of scale.

## Tailoring and compression rules

- Delete any conditional slide whose answer cannot change the thesis, plan, valuation, terms, financing, or action.
- Combine slides only when the combined page preserves one action title, one dominant proof, readable evidence, and a clear implication.
- Split a page when it contains two independent conclusions, two unrelated evidence systems, or an exhibit that becomes unreadable at presentation scale.
- Keep detailed calculations, account lists, interview records, and model schedules in the appendix; keep the core page that states the decision-relevant conclusion.
- Do not create empty customer, supplier, technology, ESG, or operations chapters merely because a generic report outline names them.
- Keep the executive synthesis, chapter conclusions, independent plan, final recommendation, and close semantically aligned after every sequence change.

## Design requirements for the complete deck

- Use one design mode, one tracker system, one section-treatment grammar, one typography hierarchy, one component-primary swatch for all non-chart emphasis, registered neutral guideline, divider, and chart-segment roles, and a chart-only palette used only when the data requires distinct series.
- Use action titles on analytical pages; title the conclusion, not the topic or exhibit type.
- Give every analytical page one dominant visual field and enough evidence, scope, comparison, and decision context to work as an executive pre-read without narration.
- Prefer direct labels, aligned comparison structures, visible units, and explicit sources over legends, decorative icons, large empty regions, or repeated metric cards.
- Use color only for stable semantic roles, data-required categories, or status. Comparable chart, table, timeline, metric, and panel items share one base role unless a direct label names the exception.
- Reserve one implication or action region per page. State the action and the condition, evidence gap, or stop trigger governing it; do not repeat the action title as a slogan or add a second callout bar.
- Keep analytical headers, table headers, and implication regions consistent. Follow [`../components/guidelines.md`](../components/guidelines.md), [`../components/copy.md`](../components/copy.md), and [`../design/index.md`](../design/index.md).

## Failure checks

- the deck follows a generic consulting workplan rather than a decision-led thesis;
- a standard or unqualified commercial-DD request is silently delivered as a red-flag review or preliminary public-source screen;
- the document claims to be full commercial DD without evidence-backed conclusions across all five analytical tests, an independent base case, decision-relevant downside, and an integrated recommendation;
- the template-coverage ledger is missing, or a core job is marked complete by a missing-data statement rather than its required analysis;
- the deck lacks a standalone executive synthesis, full contents tracker, material chapter-transition states, branch conclusions, or an evidence-backed close;
- an analytical slide does not map to a hypothesis, dot, title, evidence package, archetype, and composition;
- market, customer, competitor, commercial-engine, and forecast pages sit beside each other without a causal bridge;
- the market thesis depends on total market growth without a defined reachable segment or plausible capture mechanism;
- customer anecdotes, survey percentages, or expert opinions are treated as representative without population, recruitment, sample, and inference limits;
- retention, churn, acquisition cost, lifetime value, pipeline, share, or margin metrics appear without definitions and reconciliations;
- competitive claims lack win, loss, switching, pricing, substitute, or customer-choice evidence;
- commercial-engine observations do not reconcile to plan drivers, capacity, or unit economics;
- management's forecast is repeated without an independent driver bridge;
- scenarios use arbitrary percentage changes without named triggers and decision consequences;
- buyer synergies compensate for a weak standalone commercial case;
- risks are listed but do not change the valuation, terms, financing, remaining diligence, ownership, timing, or decision;
- the close introduces a condition, claim, or action that was not established earlier;
- a page is visually sparse because evidence, scope, unresolved tests, or decision context were omitted;
- a page is visually dense because multiple communication jobs were forced into one slide;
- decorative maps, funnels, ecosystems, scorecards, logos, gauges, or dashboards substitute for evidence; or
- arbitrary color, mixed tracker systems, mixed analytical-header treatments, repeated slogans, ornamental edge stripes, or competing callout regions weaken the hierarchy.

## Acceptance check

Before release, verify that the declared engagement mode matches the title, evidence base, analytical coverage, and strength of recommendation; the template-coverage ledger accounts for every core job; the deck answers the root question, preserves the approved dot-dash, includes every material branch and no empty branch, states the independent base case and decision-relevant downside, reconciles every important metric and model bridge, makes research limits visible, routes every page to a defined design, uses one coherent component system, and ends with a recommendation and actions already supported by the evidence.
