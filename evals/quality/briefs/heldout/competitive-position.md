# Brief: Harbourline or Kestrel Freight Software

A logistics investor is deciding which of two mid-sized freight-software vendors
is better placed to win mid-market shippers over the next three years. Both are
private; the only evidence is what each company gave the investor, below.

- **Audience.** An investment committee of five, reading ahead of a meeting.
- **Decision.** Which company to take forward to full diligence, and what would
  reverse that choice.
- **Ranked criteria.** Retention of paying customers; growth in the mid-market
  segment; product breadth against what mid-market shippers buy; cost to win a
  customer.
- **Constraints.** Use only the figures below. The two companies define "active
  customer" differently (Harbourline: invoiced in the last 90 days; Kestrel: any
  signed contract) and the deck must say what that does to each comparison.
  Kestrel's fiscal year ends in March, Harbourline's in December. Say which
  conclusions are judgement rather than measurement.
- **Length.** About 20 pages.

| Measure | Harbourline (FY Dec 2025) | Kestrel (FY Mar 2026) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Revenue | $48m | $61m |
| Revenue growth | 31% | 18% |
| Mid-market share of revenue | 64% | 38% |
| Net revenue retention | 112% | 97% |
| Gross logo churn | 9% | 14% |
| Active customers | 1,140 | 2,020 |
| Modules sold (of 6 in the category) | 3 | 6 |
| Sales and marketing spend | $19m | $17m |
| New customers in the year | 310 | 420 |
| Median implementation time | 6 weeks | 14 weeks |

Kestrel also reports that 41% of its revenue comes from its ten largest
customers; Harbourline did not answer the same question.

Why this brief is in the suite: it is held out. A two-company comparison on a
small table of unequal definitions is the page the skill is tuned against in
the development set, and this one checks that the tuning generalises: bases
reconciled before comparing, the missing concentration figure left visible as
missing, and a recommendation with its reversal condition.
