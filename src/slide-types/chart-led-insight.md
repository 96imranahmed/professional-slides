# Chart-Led Insight

## Use when

A quantitative pattern is the strongest proof of the slide's claim: magnitude,
trend, composition, change, distribution, or relationship.

Do not use a chart when exact lookup, ordered steps, or a qualitative causal
structure is the real task.

## Narrative contract

The audience should see the pattern quickly, understand measure and scope, and
know why it changes the decision.

## Content contract

- action title stating the finding;
- optional metric/scope/unit/period subtitle;
- one primary chart or coherent small-multiple set;
- direct annotation on decisive evidence;
- implication or takeaway rail when the conclusion needs explanation;
- source and calculation provenance.

## Layout

- chart occupies roughly 60% to 85% of the body;
- optional takeaway rail uses the remaining width;
- source sits in the consistent footer zone;
- legends and annotations stay inside defined chart bounds.

Use full width when the chart is self-explanatory. Use a takeaway rail when the
audience needs drivers, implications, or decision context.

## Visual rules

- Choose the chart through [the chart router](../charts/index.md).
- Use neutral context and one accent for the decisive evidence.
- Remove redundant borders, legends, gridlines, ticks, and decimals.
- Direct-label highlighted series where practical.
- Use honest axes and clearly distinguish actual/forecast/target/scenario.
- Keep labels readable after native platform conversion.

## Variants

- full-width chart;
- two-thirds chart plus one-third takeaway rail;
- small multiples with shared scales;
- chart plus metric/calculation bridge;
- chart above a narrow implications strip.

## Failure modes

- a chart that merely repeats the title;
- misleading baseline or non-comparable scales;
- too many colors or series;
- unsupported dual-axis relationship;
- chart screenshots when editable native charts are feasible;
- data labels that do not match source values.

## Acceptance test

Cover the title: the visual pattern should be visible. Cover the chart: the
title should state the conclusion. Reveal both: they should prove the same
claim.
