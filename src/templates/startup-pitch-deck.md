# Startup Pitch Deck

## Primary decision

Use for investors deciding whether a startup warrants a meeting, further diligence, or funding and whether the proposed round can support the next value-creation milestone.

The root question is: **Why should this company win now, what evidence shows it can become a valuable business, and what will the requested capital prove or unlock?**

## Thesis contract

The governing thought must connect an urgent customer problem, a differentiated solution, evidence of demand, a credible scaling mechanism, and the purpose of the raise. A pitch is not coherent when these appear as separate company facts without one causal investment thesis.

## Default hypothesis branches

1. **Problem and timing:** a valuable customer problem exists and market or technology conditions make the opportunity urgent now.
2. **Solution and advantage:** the product solves the problem materially better and has a credible source of differentiation or learning advantage.
3. **Demand proof:** adoption, engagement, retention, revenue, pipeline, customer evidence, or validated milestones demonstrate real pull at the company's stage.
4. **Scale model:** market reach, business model, go-to-market motion, economics, and roadmap can compound into a large outcome.
5. **Team and raise:** the team is unusually suited to execute and the requested capital funds explicit milestones that reduce the next major risks.

## Chapter and tracker contract

Use these exact labels by default: **Problem and timing**, **Solution and advantage**, **Demand proof**, **Scale model**, **Team and raise**. Short live pitches may establish this map on a compact contents or chapter-heading page and use only restrained running labels afterwards; omit persistent navigation only when the whole deck remains short enough that orientation is genuinely unnecessary. Do not replace the tracker with decorative section names or a generic `Company`, `Market`, `Product`, `Team` sequence that obscures the investment logic.

## Required storyline jobs

- open with the investment thesis rather than a slogan or product description;
- make the problem concrete for a defined customer and explain why current alternatives fail;
- show the product mechanism and differentiation without turning the pitch into a feature catalogue;
- use stage-appropriate proof and distinguish actual results from targets, projections, pilots, and anecdotes;
- connect market opportunity to the go-to-market and economic model rather than presenting an isolated total-addressable-market number;
- show how team capability and the use of funds address the critical execution risks;
- close with the round, intended milestones, and the next investor action.

## Evidence obligations

Use the strongest available evidence for customer pain, product behavior, retention or engagement, revenue or pipeline, market reach, competition, pricing, acquisition, unit economics, technical or data advantage, team fit, capital needs, and milestones. When a metric is unavailable at the company's stage, state the next falsifiable test rather than filling the space with vanity metrics or generic vision copy.

## Failure checks

- the deck follows a conventional pitch order but does not express one investment thesis;
- the opening is a tagline that does not identify the customer, problem, or reason to believe;
- the product section is a feature tour without proof of superior outcomes;
- traction uses cumulative, vanity, or pipeline figures without cohorts, conversion, or scope;
- market size is disconnected from the initial segment and go-to-market motion;
- the raise is a budget list rather than a set of risk-reducing milestones;
- the close repeats the mission but does not ask for a specific next action.
