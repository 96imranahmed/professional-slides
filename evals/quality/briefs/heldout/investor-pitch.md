# Brief: Tidewell seed extension pitch

The founders of Tidewell, a fictional start-up that runs battery-swap stations
for electric cargo bikes used by urban delivery fleets, are raising a $4m seed
extension and want the pitch deck.

- **Audience.** Early-stage investors in a first meeting, twenty minutes, deck
  on screen; the same deck is sent afterwards.
- **Decision.** Whether to take a second meeting.
- **Constraints.** Every figure below is from the company's own systems and is
  unaudited; say so once, plainly. The pilot is two cities, so do not present
  city-level unit economics as proven at scale. Market size must be built from
  the numbers given, not from an external headline figure.
- **Length.** 12 to 15 pages.

What the founders gave you:

- Pilot in two cities since March 2025: 38 stations, 610 bikes on the network,
  9 fleet customers, 3 of whom signed after a paid trial.
- Swaps per station per day: 21 in the older city, 12 in the newer one.
- Price $2.10 per swap; energy and station upkeep $0.95 per swap; station
  capex $11,000, expected life six years.
- Monthly recurring revenue $43,000 in August 2026, up from $9,000 in
  August 2025.
- Fleet customer churn: one of 10 customers left in twelve months.
- Target: 14 cities within three years; the founders estimate 6,500 cargo bikes
  in delivery fleets across those cities today.
- Use of funds: $2.6m stations, $0.9m hires (operations and sales), $0.5m
  software.

Why this brief is in the suite: it is held out. A pitch is where the skill is
tempted to trade evidence for adjectives. A good run shows the unit economics
per station and per swap with their pilot basis, builds the market from the
fleet count rather than asserting it, and ends on the ask and what it buys.
