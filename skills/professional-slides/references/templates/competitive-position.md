# Competitive position

## Mandate

Use when the reader asks who is better positioned - two or more companies, products, locations or options - now, over a longer horizon, or on a named dimension such as capital or distribution. The deck makes a directional call on every question asked. Name the horizon for each ("the next 12 months", "by 2030") and the set compared; a comparison of two players states why the rest of the field is out of scope.

## Decision question

> Who is better positioned on the criteria that decide this market, by how much, how sure are we, and what would change the call?

## Page table

| Page | Job | Exhibit | Evidence it owes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cover | Name the players, the horizon and the evidence cutoff | Cover with the players' marks (logos, or outlines where the players are places) | Evidence date, the set compared |
| Executive summary | The call on each question asked, with its confidence and reversal trigger | Executive synthesis; a scorecard of the call when it carries the answer | Every sub-question in the request, each with a lean |
| Players | Who the players are, with the numbers the deck compares | `profiles` (logos or logo table, each player by the mark it declares) | Size, business model, the products compared |
| Criteria and weights | What decides the market and how much each criterion counts | `lookup` or `scorecard` of criteria, weight and why | Why each criterion matters to buyers, investors or users; how the weights were set |
| One section per criterion | Who leads on it, by how much, and whether the gap is closing | Trend with rates, the whole peer set ranked, share shifting, a map | The same measure for every player, dated and sourced; `n/a` where a player does not publish it |
| Scorecard | Every player scored on every criterion, weighted | `scorecard` (harvey, heatmap or bars) with the weighted total | Each score traced to its section's evidence |
| The call | Who is better positioned, now and over the horizon | Scorecard total, or a decision table of the call per question | The lean, its confidence, the reversal trigger, what to watch |
| Countercase | The strongest case for the other player | `options` or labelled rows | The evidence that argues against the call, and why it does not reverse it |
| Appendix | Method, source record, reference schedules | Tables | Source status (audited, company-reported, press, estimate), definitions |

## Evidence

Score every player on every criterion from the same kind of evidence, on the same basis and period. Weight the criteria before scoring, and record why. A score cites its section's exhibit; a criterion with no comparable evidence for a player is scored on the best proxy with a low confidence, not left blank. Separate company-reported figures from audited and third-party ones in the `sources` registry's `status`, and carry that status into the citation line.

## Failure checks

Reject when: a question in the request has no call; more than one question is left unranked, or the one unranked question does not name the evidence that would settle it; titles state what the evidence lacks instead of which way it leans; the criteria were chosen after the scores or carry no weights; a player is compared on a measure the others are not; the scorecard total contradicts the sections; the countercase is missing.

## Acceptance check

Every question in the request carries a lean with its magnitude, confidence and reversal trigger; each criterion is a section whose title states who leads; the weighted scorecard reproduces from the sections; the call follows from the scorecard; the countercase is answered.
