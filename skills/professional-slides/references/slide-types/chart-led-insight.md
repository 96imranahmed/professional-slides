# Chart-Led Insight

## Use when

A quantitative pattern is the strongest proof of the slide's claim: magnitude, trend, composition, change, distribution, or relationship. The chart should carry the page with only concise framing and annotation.

Use [`argument with chart`](argument-with-chart.md) when a verbal mechanism or qualification must remain a coequal part of the page. Use [`text-led implication`](text-led-implication.md) when the reasoning and consequence matter but no chart is required. Do not use a chart when exact lookup, ordered steps, or a qualitative causal structure is the real task.

## Narrative contract

The audience should see one quantitative pattern quickly, understand its measure and scope, and know why it changes the decision. Several charts may contribute to that pattern, but they must function as one evidence system rather than independent mini-slides.

## Content contract

- optional metric/scope/unit/period subtitle;
- one primary chart or two to four coordinated charts selected through one [evidence composition](evidence-compositions.md);
- direct annotation on decisive evidence;
- concise implication annotation only when the action title and highlighted evidence cannot carry the decision consequence.

## Layout

- the quantitative evidence field occupies roughly 75% to 100% of the analytical body unless the selected `parallel evidence with synthesis` variant reserves a theme-defined insight region;
- source sits in the consistent footer zone;
- legends and annotations stay inside the evidence field or its explicitly attached synthesis region.

Use full width when one chart is self-explanatory. Use the [parallel evidence field](evidence-compositions.md#parallel-evidence-field) for peer charts, [parallel evidence with synthesis](evidence-compositions.md#parallel-evidence-with-synthesis) for peer charts plus a concise implication rail, and a [progressive evidence path](evidence-compositions.md#progressive-evidence-path) only when the charts build one directional quantitative proof. If explanatory prose supplies a mechanism required to interpret the charts, route to [`argument with chart`](argument-with-chart.md).

## Visual rules

- Choose the chart through [the chart router](../charts/index.md).
- Align peer plot areas, share scales and legends where the measures are comparable, and preserve one series-colour mapping across every panel.
- Remove redundant borders, legends, gridlines, ticks, and decimals.
- Direct-label highlighted series where practical.
- Use honest axes and clearly distinguish actual/forecast/target/scenario.
- Keep labels readable after native platform conversion.
- Apply the [`visible label gate`](../components/copy.md#visible-label-gate) to every attached synthesis region. Do not title it “What it means,” “Read the outliers,” “Insight,” or “Synthesis”; state the interpretation or action directly.

## Failure modes

- a chart that merely repeats the title;
- misleading baseline or non-comparable scales;
- too many colors or series;
- unsupported dual-axis relationship;
- unrelated charts arranged as a dashboard;
- panel headings that compete as separate action titles;
- an insight rail that repeats visible values instead of synthesizing their consequence;
- chart screenshots when editable native charts are feasible;
- data labels that do not match source values.

## Acceptance test

Cover the title: the visual pattern should be visible across the complete evidence field. Cover every chart or panel: the title should state the same conclusion. Hide one panel at a time: every retained panel must remove a necessary clause of proof. Reveal all: the evidence system and title should make one claim.
